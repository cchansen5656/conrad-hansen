Overview

The short version

I was among the first people RecNation hired, and that timing shaped how I work. When you join a company before the playbook exists, you don't inherit processes — you write them, watch them break under load, and rewrite them. I have been doing that since I finished college, which has made me considerably more comfortable with ambiguity than most people five years into a career.

Today I serve as Regional Manager for the West Region, covering Texas, California, and Arizona. The role is operations in the fullest sense: occupancy and revenue performance, site-level staffing and development, vendor and capital projects, and the continuous work of ensuring a facility in Sacramento and a facility outside Houston are run to the same standard despite sharing almost nothing else.

I have worked across several departments along the way, which has proven the most useful line on my résumé. I understand how the functions connect — and, more importantly, where they stop connecting when something goes wrong.

Trajectory

Where I am headed

My objective is to run operations at the enterprise level, ultimately as a Chief Operating Officer in real estate. The path there has less to do with titles than with scope: full P&L ownership, leading leaders rather than operators, and becoming fluent enough in capital to sit in an operations review in the morning and a lender conversation in the afternoon without any loss of credibility.

Most operators stall because they only ever learn half the business. I would rather learn the other half deliberately than discover later that I needed it.
