Background

I build the systems a growing company runs on.

Five years, one company, three states, and a front-row seat to what happens when a business scales faster than its processes.

The short version

I was one of the first people RecNation hired. That timing shaped everything about how I work. When you join a company before the playbook exists, you don't inherit processes — you write them, watch them break under load, and rewrite them. I've been doing that since I came out of college, and it's made me far more comfortable with ambiguity than most people five years into a career.

Today I'm Regional Manager for the West Region, which covers Texas, California, and Arizona. The job is operations in the truest sense: occupancy and revenue performance, site-level staffing and development, vendor and capital projects, and the constant work of making sure a facility in Sacramento and a facility outside Houston are run to the same standard despite having almost nothing else in common.

Along the way I've worked across multiple departments, which turns out to be the most useful thing on my résumé. I know how the pieces connect — and more importantly, I know where they stop connecting when something goes wrong.

Where I'm headed

I want to run operations at the company level — eventually as a COO in real estate. The path there is less about titles than about scope: full P&L ownership, leading leaders instead of operators, and getting fluent enough in capital that I can sit in an operations review in the morning and a lender conversation in the afternoon without changing my level of credibility.

Most operators stall because they only ever learn half the business. I'd rather learn the other half on purpose than find out later I needed it.
